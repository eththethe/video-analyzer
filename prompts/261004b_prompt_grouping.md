Every input video is eligible for selection. The cards deliberately omit

PLAYBOOK: WHAT OUR WINNING FACEBOOK ADS SHARE
Source: 204 Sahiyya+ Facebook ads with ROAS (160 from 2026 Q1-Q3, 44 from
2025). "Hit" = ROAS >= 3. These are associations in our own data, not laws;
2025 logged winners only, so medians are the fairer comparison.

The formula, one ad in six parts:
- Hook: reveal, don't scare. Silent danger, a symptom, or the viewer's own
  doubt said out loud. (Silent danger 13 of 13; stroke-burden fear 0 of 3.)
- Proof: a reading or a number: eGFR, stage, "gula 10.2 -> 5.2", "90 hari".
  (With a reading 89% hit vs 81% without.)
- Close: name a person, one button. ("Iqa / Ika / tim kami akan bantu semak":
  27 of 27 hit; at MOFU 16 of 16, median ROAS 5.86.) Add a real reason (price
  returns, stock) only at BOFU.
- Caption opener: a symptom or stage line, even when the video is a
  testimonial (testimonial video + symptom/stage caption 18 of 18).
- Caption body: under 160 words, 0-2 freebies, COD in the body, no
  "99 orang terawal".
- Headline: a person or the hook ("Klik WhatsApp Tanya Dulu >>", ROAS 17.56),
  not "Barang sampai baru bayar".

Eight patterns that hold across every disease:
1. Name a person in the close.
2. One button, said the same way everywhere (video, caption and button all
   WhatsApp, or all Shop Now). Clean WhatsApp 48 of 51; video and caption
   disagreeing 17 of 25.
3. Drop "99 orang terawal": in 55% of misses vs 25% of the top 20.
4. Reveal, don't threaten: "senyap-senyap rosak" works; "kalau saya lumpuh,
   siapa nak mandikan saya?" does not.
5. Put proof in numbers.
6. Doctor lines need a twist: "Doktor tengok report, lepas tu dia diam"
   (ROAS 9.81) beats "Doktor sahkan buah pinggang bocor".
7. Testimonial video, symptom caption.
8. Seasonal ads need a price or stock reason, never gift-only (event +
   stock/price warning 4 of 4, median 7.78; event + gift 1 of 4).

Video types, ranked:
- Tier 1 (go-to, most breakout ads): animated mascot or cartoon (5 of 5,

  presenter pitches the bundle (12 of 14, BOFU).
- Tier 2 (reliable, rarely break out): presenter retells a customer's result
  (MOFU); presenter replies to a comment (MOFU); "ori dari HQ?" check (BOFU);
  real customer testimonial (17 of 18, none above ROAS 7: needs a strong
  hook); product B-roll with text (BOFU promo); static poster.
- Tier 3 (low ceiling or risky): celebrity (all hit, none broke out);
  AI-generated realistic person (7 of 11; AI elderly people 1 of 5);
  event or booth crowd as the main footage (2 of 4).
Winning spec: 9:16, 30-60 s (under 30 s fine for straight promos), a face or
character talking at 0.5 s, hook headline on screen at 0.5 s, word-by-word
subtitles, product on screen in the first 3 s, a CTA end card with the same
button word as the voice. Over 90 s: 25 of 33.

Stop doing: "99 orang terawal" as the close; video says Shop Now while the
caption says WhatsApp; "Barang sampai baru bayar" as the headline; the
stroke-burden fear story; gift-only seasonal closes.

CORE DISTINCTIONS
1. Topic = broad subject, e.g. kidney health or product authenticity.
2. Concept = a specific central argument or story and intended takeaway.
3. Variant = another execution of substantially the same concept.
4. Persuasion role = the belief, concern or action the creative addresses.
5. Ad set = a delivery group of exactly 5 ads that share a buyer condition
   and a conversion goal.

STEP 1 - CLASSIFY EACH VIDEO
Use the actual description, not just its title or inherited funnel label.
For each file identify:
- Topic, one-sentence core takeaway, intended buyer, prior product knowledge.
- Primary funnel stage (TOFU / MOFU / BOFU, or unclear); secondary stage only
  when justified. Judge the stage from the viewer decision the video serves,
  using the playbook's stage definitions above.
- Disease focus in playbook terms: buah_pinggang, 3_serangkai,
  kencing_manis, kolesterol, jantung_salur_darah, or general_promo.
  Darah tinggi counts as 3_serangkai; product authenticity, packages, price,
  usage and seller trust count as general_promo.
- Playbook hook pattern, close pattern (and whether the close names a person),
  and video type, using the playbook's categories.
- Playbook fit: which of the formula parts and eight patterns it follows,
  which "stop doing" items it contains, and whether its hook/close pairing
  is a proven one.
- Primary persuasion role and at most two secondary roles.
- The objection or belief it addresses, in the customer's words.
- Main promise, proof, offer, CTA and destination if known.
- Format, presenter and emotional approach; whether it stands alone.
- Concerns: weak hook, missing or mismatched CTA, length, format tier or
  other creative problems. Not claim verification.

Suggested persuasion roles, only when supported:
A. Relevance: "Why should I pay attention?"
B. Understanding: "What do I need to understand?"
C. Differentiation: "Why consider Sahiyya instead of another option?"
D. Credibility: "What supports this claim?"
E. Authenticity/service: "Is this seller genuine? Who helps after payment?"
F. Suitability/use: "Is this appropriate for me? How is it used?"
G. Value/quantity: "Why this price or package? Why two bottles?"
H. Action: "What should I do next, and why now?"

STEP 2 - BUILD CONCEPT FAMILIES AND IDENTIFY REDUNDANCY
Assign every input video to exactly one concept.
- Re-edits, cut-downs and hook variants of the same central argument normally
  belong together. A one-video concept is valid.
- Shared topics, presenters, footage or series branding alone do not make
  videos the same concept.
Distinguish exact/near duplicates, meaningful variants worth testing, and
complementary concepts. For duplicates, name a preferred launch version and
reserve versions, with evidence and confidence. If only descriptions are
available, label suspected duplicates as unverified.

STEP 3 - BUILD THE AD SETS
Ad set rules:
- Every ad set holds exactly 5 ads.
- Build at least 5 ad sets, so at least 25 videos launch. Add more ad sets
  only when the library has enough strong, distinct material to fill them
  without weak picks; most libraries this size support 5 to 8.
- Stage mix per ad set, allocated from the playbook: 1 TOFU, 2 MOFU, 2 BOFU.
  Why: the playbook's stage-tagged winners are mostly MOFU (69 ads, 58 hit)
  and BOFU (57 ads, 51 hit); TOFU is a perfect but thin record (14 of 14).
  Per 5 ads that is about 20% TOFU, 40% MOFU, 40% BOFU, keeping one TOFU
  entry point for viewers who still feel fine.
  If one ad set's disease genuinely lacks a usable video for a stage, you may
  swap that slot to the neighbouring stage (TOFU <-> MOFU, MOFU <-> BOFU) and
  say why. Never fill an ad set from a single stage, and never use fewer
  than two stages in one ad set.
- Each ad's slot stage must equal its primary stage from STEP 1.
- Organise ad sets by disease focus, because the playbook shows the winning
  hooks and closes differ by disease: e.g. one buah_pinggang ad set, one
  kencing_manis ad set, one general_promo ad set. A disease with deep
  material may get two ad sets with different angles; thin diseases may share
  one ad set (e.g. kolesterol with jantung_salur_darah) when the buyers and
  the close are compatible.
- Within an ad set, pick complementary arguments, not five versions of one
  idea: at most one near-duplicate per concept, and cover different
  persuasion roles (relevance at TOFU; understanding, credibility and
  suitability at MOFU; differentiation, authenticity, value and action at
  BOFU), as far as the material allows.
- Prefer, in order: the #1-3 hook pattern for that disease and stage; a
  proven hook/close pairing; a close that names a person; Tier 1 and Tier 2
  video types; proof shown as a reading or number; the winning video spec.
  Use the creative score and section scores to choose between otherwise
  equal candidates. A high creative score is not proof of performance.
- Do not select fear-of-burden stories, gift-only seasonal closes or
  AI-generated elderly testimonials while alternatives exist.
- A video may be used in more than one ad set when it fits each ad set's
  disease focus and its stage slot there; reuse your strongest videos rather
  than filling a slot with a weak one. Within one ad set, never use the same
  file twice.
- Keep Sahiyya and Sahiyya Plus product facts and offers distinct, and avoid
  contradictory prices, instructions or promises within one ad set.
- Explain why each ad earns its slot and what it adds beyond the other four.

STEP 4 - DELIVERY AND COPY GUIDANCE PER AD SET
For each ad set give the campaign purpose, provisional audience (inclusions,
lookback windows, exclusions; state them as provisional), objective/event,
budget approach and why it is separate. Do not invent budgets or audience
sizes. Explain that ordinary delivery does not guarantee each person sees all
5 ads, sees them equally or in order; each ad must work on its own.
Give copy guidance from the playbook for the ad set: a caption opener type
(symptom or stage line, even for testimonials), the CTA line (one button,
named person), and a headline type (a person/"tanya dulu" or the hook, not
"Barang sampai baru bayar"), each with a short Malay example.

STEP 5 - GAPS AND MEASUREMENT
Identify the highest-priority gaps against the playbook: proven patterns with
no usable video in this library for a disease and stage (the playbook's
"mirror map": e.g. kolesterol x silent danger, kencing manis x family
testimonial, an animated mascot for kencing manis or kolesterol). For each,
give a NEW CONTENT brief: audience, belief change, required proof, hook line
in Malay, close and CTA. Rank by likely purchase impact; mark it a hypothesis.
Give a short measurement plan: purchases, qualified enquiries, CPA and ROAS
(hit = ROAS >= 3, as in the playbook) by ad and by stage slot; diagnostics
(click rate, retention, conversion rate); delivery (spend, impressions,
frequency per ad); and evidence -> action rules without invented thresholds.
